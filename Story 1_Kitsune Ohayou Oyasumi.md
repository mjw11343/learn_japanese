# 📖 Story: Little Kitsune’s Morning and Night

---

In the small forest, a **fox** lived. This **fox**’s name was **Kitsune (狐)**.  
**Fox** — 狐 (きつね, *kitsune*) — *literal: fox*

Every morning, **Kitsune (狐)** liked to say **“Good morning” — おはよう (*Ohayou*) — literal: good morning**.

When the sun rose, **Kitsune (狐)** would open eyes and whisper, **“Good morning” — おはよう (*Ohayou*) — literal: good morning**.

**Kitsune (狐)** liked how **おはよう (*Ohayou*)** felt. It made the forest warm.

---

In the evening, the stars came.  
When the moon was high, **Kitsune (狐)** said **“Good night” — おやすみ (*Oyasumi*) — literal: good rest**.

Closing eyes, **Kitsune (狐)** whispered again, **“Good night” — おやすみ (*Oyasumi*) — literal: good rest**.

In dreams, **Kitsune (狐)** slept softly, saying one more time: **おやすみ (*Oyasumi*) — literal: good rest**.

---

Every day, **Kitsune (狐)** woke up with **おはよう (*Ohayou*)** and slept with **おやすみ (*Oyasumi*)**.

---

In the forest, **Kitsune (狐)** taught **おはよう (*Ohayou*)** to the birds.  
The birds chirped **おはよう (*Ohayou*)** when the dawn came.

At night, the owls hooted softly, **おやすみ (*Oyasumi*)** before sleep.

---

So the forest sang:  
**Morning — おはよう (*Ohayou*)**  
**Night — おやすみ (*Oyasumi*)**  
**Fox — 狐 (きつね, *kitsune*)**

---

**Kitsune (狐)** smiled, happy to know **おはよう (*Ohayou*)** and **おやすみ (*Oyasumi*)**.

---

| Fox
狐
(kitsune) | fox | 犭 (kemono-hen): animal / beast radical.
Learning tip: This is a stylized version of 犬 (dog) compressed to fit on the left, indicating that the character represents a wild, four-legged mammal.

瓜 (uri): melon | Phono-semantic compound (形声文字).

The left side (犭) provides the meaning category (a wild animal), while the right side (瓜) provides the original Chinese/Sino-Japanese phonetic sound (ko). | Picture a sly beast (犭) sneaking into a farmer's field to steal a melon (瓜). |
| Good morning
おはよう / お早う
(ohayou) | "It is early" | お (o-): honorific prefix

早 (haya): early
- 日 (nichi): sun
- 十 (juu): cross / stem
Learning tip: The bottom cross is actually a stylized simplification of an ancient pictograph showing a small grass sprout pushing out of the earth beneath the morning sun. | Grammatical shift & Ideograph.

Derived from the adverb hayaku shifting into hayou for easier pronunciation. The kanji 早 is an ideograph (指事文字) representing a concept through a combined picture. | Respectfully acknowledging someone waking early, just as the sun (日) rises over a flower stem (十). |
| Good night
おやすみ / お休み
(oyasumi) | "Rest" | お (o-): honorific prefix

休 (yasu): rest
- 亻 (ninben): person radical.
Learning tip: This is a stylized, compressed version of 人 (person). It is flattened to fit on the left, immediately signaling that the kanji relates to human actions or states.
- 木 (ki): tree | Compound ideograph (会意文字).

A noun derived from the verb 休む (yasumu, to rest). The kanji 休 combines two distinct meaning-based components to create a brand-new concept (person + tree = rest). | Imagine a tired person (亻) leaning against a tree (木) to take a well-deserved rest for the night. |
